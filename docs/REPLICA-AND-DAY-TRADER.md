# Replica and Day Trader projects

**Replica** helps someone turn a useful behavior they have seen into an original product specification. **Day Trader** helps someone practice decisions about historical price movements. They share Studio's project picker, horizontally sliding sheets, revisioned drafts and local export. The two projects have different outputs and different rules about what an agent can do.

## Replica

Imagine a volunteer coordinator who likes how a task list updates its open count. Their version needs one addition: a way to hand a task to the next shift. The first useful result is a one-page spec another person can implement and test. A local prototype has a separate M6B acceptance gate.

| Sheet | Main decision | Detail on demand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reference** | Describe the behavior in your own words, paste a source locator, or start with a Studio example. Identify what you have permission to analyze. | Record source revision, observed conditions and permission to retain or distribute attachments separately. An inaccessible link stays a note. |
| **Brief** | Choose the audience and the one action the new product should help them finish. Ask the agent to propose two approaches and explain which observation supports each. | Keep observations, attributed feedback and hypotheses distinct. Write a behavioral test, such as “complete one of three open tasks and the count becomes two.” |
| **Design** | Describe an original screen and one improvement. Review the empty, completed and interrupted states. | Inspect a flow map, data fields, accessibility behavior and unresolved questions. Create original assets and copy, and link each test to the intended behavior. |
| **Export** | Save the brief, original design and acceptance criteria so a collaborator can build from them. | Inspect the handoff contents. Include source evidence or attachments only when their terms allow redistribution. Preserve private provenance separately. |
| **Prototype, optional** | At M6B, build a small isolated prototype from the reviewed specification. | Compare its behavior against the user's test cases, inspect original source and verify offline reopen. A visual resemblance score cannot satisfy a functional test. |

The upstream MIT-licensed pack has eleven skills. Its observation, architecture and design stages inform the brief. Its build, backend, test and comparison stages inform implementation and acceptance. Its review-mining, brand and launch stages fit Studio's existing research and launch modules. The upstream deploy step needs separate destination authority in Studio. ## Day Trader

The planned first replay uses one fictional symbol and one small, redistributable set of price bars. The learner commits a hypothetical decision before revealing the next bar, sees how the simulator handled it and writes what they would change. A decision to pass is a valid journal entry. The working-draft guide can record a practice plan and manual notes before replay is available.

| Sheet | Main decision | Detail on demand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Practice data** | Choose the included exercise or identify historical data you are permitted to use. | Show symbol, session, interval, timezone, adjustment basis, license and content digest. Flag duplicate or unordered timestamps, invalid bars and missing intervals. Preserve gaps. |
| **Rules** | Write the conditions you want to practice and when you would pass. The examples use fictional balances. | For replay, pin a rule revision, starting balance, fees, currency precision, fill assumptions and simulator version. A changed rule starts a comparable new run. |
| **Replay** | Advance one completed bar at a time. Inspect only information available as of the displayed clock, then record a hypothetical decision. | In the first engine fixture, a market decision entered after a close can fill at the next eligible open. Record the modeled fee and slippage. At the end of data, record the unfilled decision. For ambiguous intrabar stop and target ordering, surface the ambiguity with a declared policy. |
| **Journal** | Compare what you expected with the simulated outcome and add a private reflection. | Distinguish a manual note, a generated explanation and an engine-produced fill. Explain the reason for a rejected or unfilled decision. Preserve the inputs and decision sequence needed to replay it. |
| **Export** | Save your notes and, after replay qualification, the journal and replay manifest. | Export totals with their calculation basis. Exclude raw bars when redistribution rights do not cover them. Reopen with matching data and recover after an interruption without duplicating a fill. |

The upstream MIT-licensed pack has eleven skills covering scans, news, entry/exit, simulation, risk, budget, broker execution, alerts, earnings and congressional disclosures. Its broker skill defaults to paper and also describes live routing. Studio uses replay and journaling as the product boundary for this release. Broker keys, live endpoints, real orders and automated trading have no route from the builder, agent, MCP or RPC. Personal investing information is unnecessary. Agents can explain an assumption using the visible exercise data. Study results identify their hypothetical fill model and costs. WP41 owns the separate M6B capability `cap.trading.replay`. A fixed exercise must produce the same journal and balances from the same input actions across restart. Mutate a future bar and prove that earlier decisions, explanations and fills stay identical. Reprocess the same action ID and prove it cannot create a second fill. Record the exact tested build, data digest and fill model. Independently review the calculations and have a novice complete a session with networking disabled. External market feeds, broker access and paid data agreements are unnecessary for this gate. [Roadmap](ROADMAP.md) · [Work packages](WORK-PACKAGES.md)
